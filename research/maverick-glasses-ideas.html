Everysight Maverick AI / AI Pro · SDK project slate

Ten things worth building for Maverick

Five AI projects and five AR projects for Maverick AI glasses, each with a mockup of the in-lens display. They use what the hardware does well: a bright full-colour display, the camera as an AI sensor, eye tracking on the Pro, and a phone that does the heavy work.

DisplaySony full-colour micro-OLED, >5000 nits, 22° field of view
Weight and battery47 g, 8+ hours with the display on, IP55, takes prescription lenses
CameraAn AI sensor that streams frames to your app. It is not meant for photos or video.
Eye trackingAI Pro only (GazeIntent). Early hands-on reviews say it is sensitive to fit.
SDKKotlin Multiplatform (Maven and Swift Package). No built-in AI, so you bring your own model or provider. Currently alpha/beta.
AccessDeveloper Edition is $399 with a 1-year licence tied to one pair. Delivery takes about 4 weeks.

The published specs don't give a pixel resolution, so the mockups are drawn to proportion, not pixel-exact. Dark areas on the micro-OLED are see-through, so every design keeps text sparse and bright. Switch to “Display only” to see just the light the glasses emit.

AI · 1

Pill Check

Hold up a pill bottle and the glasses confirm it is the right medicine and the right time. Everything is checked against your Med Counter list over Bluetooth, and you see the dose, the count left and the refill date.

camera frameson-phone OCRtouch: mark takenno cloud
Why it stands out
It ties to Med Counter, an app you're already launching, and medication safety is a serious use case for a reviewer to see.
Watch out
Read labels on the phone with no network call, so Med Counter stays 100% local. Build it as a separate companion so the main app stays free of SDKs.
First milestone
Match the label text to a local list, then show one confirmation card.

AI · 2

Look & Ask

Look at one word, part or symbol and tap. The camera frame and your gaze point go to a model, which explains that exact thing and not the whole scene. It works on manuals, error screens, wiring diagrams and foreign-language signs.

AI Pro · gaze pointcamera framestouchyour AI provider
Why it stands out
It shows off GazeIntent, Maverick's main advantage over camera-only glasses. Gaze turns a vague “what is this?” into a precise question.
Watch out
Gaze accuracy depends on fit, so crop a generous region around the gaze point and let the model pick the target.
First milestone
Gaze point plus a cropped frame, returning a two-line answer card.

Print Doctor

AI · 4

Sound Sentry

An accessibility tool for people who are deaf or hard of hearing, or anyone wearing headphones. The microphone stream runs through an on-device sound classifier and shows alarms, doorbells, a crying baby or someone saying your name as glanceable icons at the edge of view.

mic streamon-phone classifierwatch hapticall-day battery
Why it stands out
It's useful every day and works well with an 8-hour display. Accessibility apps make a strong case in an SDK application.
Watch out
Maverick probably can't tell which direction a sound came from, so give the sound type and confidence. Keep audio on the device.
First milestone
Three sound classes (smoke alarm, doorbell, your name) shown as edge chips.

AI · 5

Build Buddy

A hands-free assembly guide for PC builds, furniture or printer kits. The camera works out which step you're on, highlights the part or connector you need, and moves to the next step when it sees the current one finished.

camera framesvoice: “next”touchyour AI provider
Why it stands out
Your hands are busy and your eyes are on the parts, which is exactly the job for a heads-up display. You can try it on your own workstation build.
Watch out
Make step detection advisory. Voice or a tap always advances the step manually.
First milestone
A scripted checklist you can advance by voice, then add detection for 3 connectors.

AR projects

Projects where the overlay depends on where you look or where you are. They use head orientation, gaze, phone GPS and a paired watch, and some need no AI at all.

AR · 1

Waypoint Home

Drop a pin when you park, or share it with a friend at a festival. A floating chevron and the distance keep pointing to the target as you turn your head, so you never look down at a map.

head orientationphone GPSlow power
Why it stands out
It's simple, reliable and quick to demo. It also proves you can handle the sensor-fusion basics.
Watch out
The compass drifts near metal and in garages. Blend in the phone's heading and show the error range honestly.
First milestone
Bearing plus distance to a saved pin, refreshed once per second.

Gaze Teleprompter

Speaker notes that follow your eyes. They scroll when you reach the end of a line, pause when you look up at the audience, and pick up where you left off when you look back.

AI Pro · gazetouch: skipoptional mic for pacing

It's an original use of eye tracking that phone or camera-only glasses can't do. It could also be a paid app.

Sky Labels

Look up at night and see planets, bright stars and constellation lines labelled in place, plus satellite passes such as the ISS. A star catalogue and orbital data are bundled offline, so it works at a dark-sky site with no signal.

head orientationphone GPS + clockoffline catalogue

A colour display is a real advantage here, and the demo looks great. Black pixels stay see-through, so the real sky stays visible.
Watch out
Orientation error shows up as mislabelled stars. Label only bright objects and add a quick “centre on Polaris” calibration.

Label the 5 brightest objects, with compass heading and altitude.

Pace Ghost

Race your own best run. A ghost marker shows how far ahead or behind you are on the same route, with heart-rate zone from your watch and the next turn. Data is shown only when it matters, so the display stays clear most of the time.

phone GPSApple Watch / Wear OSIP55 · 5000 nits

For the SDK request

Paste-ready application note

I'm an independent Android developer. I'm launching Med Counter on Google Play (now in closed testing), a medication inventory and refill tracker that runs 100% on the device. I'd like Maverick AI Pro SDK access to build glanceable, privacy-first tools that only work with an in-lens display and eye tracking.

First build (4–6 weeks): Look & Ask. The user looks at a word, part or label and taps, and the app sends the GazeIntent point and a cropped camera frame to a model and shows a two-line answer. I'll also prototype Gaze Teleprompter (notes that scroll with your eyes and pause when you look up) and Pill Check (on-phone label matching against a local medication list, with no cloud).

Next: Sound Sentry (accessibility alerts from the mic) and Waypoint Home (head-orientation and GPS wayfinding). I'll share fit and gaze-accuracy findings with your team, and I'm happy to publish samples for other developers.
